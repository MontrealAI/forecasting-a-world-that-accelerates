# Interpretation guide

## What a forecast means

A scenario is a conditional mapping from stated evidence and assumptions to a distribution of outcomes. It is not a promise. The base scenario is not automatically the most likely outcome, and the discontinuous scenario is not automatically the upper bound.

## What the canonical minimum does

The minimum operator enforces two necessary conditions for realization:

- technical capacity must exist;
- sufficient demand, adoption, and utilization must exist.

The residual bottleneck factor then captures constraints not already included in either branch. Analysts must document the factor assignment and pass the double-counting audit.

## Time horizon is not runtime

A task-completion horizon measured in human-expert time represents task difficulty under a specified suite and setup. It is not the wall-clock time an agent runs, and it is not proof that all work of that duration can be automated.

## Reliability compounds across workflows

High per-step reliability can still produce low end-to-end reliability over many dependent steps. Repeated attempts can improve task success, but correlated errors, defective verification, permission failure, and shared infrastructure can invalidate independence assumptions.

## Parallelism has a floor

Parallel agents compress independent work. They do not remove serial dependencies, authority latency, external waiting, physical lead times, or coordination overhead. The critical path remains a lower bound.

## Discontinuity is conditional

The recursive-improvement regime is activated only under evidence that a verified and authorized share of the improvement loop is autonomous and that net feedback exceeds friction. The unconstrained double-exponential equation is a stress model, not a claim of infinite physical output.
