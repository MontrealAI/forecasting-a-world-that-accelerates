# Patents, publication, and prior-art record

Public release may create a dated disclosure of the material actually published, but publication is not a substitute for a patentability opinion, freedom-to-operate analysis, trademark clearance, ownership review, contract review, or filing strategy. Public disclosure can destroy or shorten patent rights in some jurisdictions.

AGPL-3.0-or-later contains the patent terms stated in section 11 for covered software. CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 is principally a copyright and related-rights license; it is not a general patent, trademark, privacy, publicity, or confidentiality license.

Before relying on publication as part of an intellectual-property strategy, obtain advice from qualified counsel familiar with the relevant jurisdictions, corporate ownership, employment and contractor agreements, prior disclosures, contributor rights, and the intended commercial model.

For defensible provenance, official releases should preserve:

- the exact Git commit and immutable version tag;
- verified commit/tag status where signing is configured;
- release archives and SHA-256 manifests;
- a GitHub release and independent archival identifier such as a Zenodo DOI;
- a public preprint identifier when issued;
- version, authorship, disclosure, license, and third-party notices;
- the complete source required to reproduce the paper and controlled experiments.

Do not claim that a local timestamp, generated checksum, repository creation date, or unsigned tag is independent proof of priority. State exactly which external records exist.
